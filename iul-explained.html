Free explainer

Protected growth and being your own bank, explained honestly.

These are the two big ideas behind indexed universal life (IUL): growth tied to the market without market losses, and borrowing against money you've built instead of from a bank. Try both below. You'll see how they work, what they cost, and who they're wrong for.

Growth linked to the market, without the market's losses

An IUL doesn't invest your money in the market. It credits interest based on how a market index performed, within limits: a floor and, on most strategies, a cap. Every IUL has a floor of at least 0%, so a down year credits nothing instead of losing value, and some companies guarantee a small minimum above that. The cap is the most it credits in a great year. Some companies offer strategies with no cap, usually in exchange for a lower participation rate or a fee. Either way, you give up some of the best years to avoid the worst ones.

What it does

Credits interest tied to an index, with a floor so market drops don't reduce your credited value.

What you give up

Gains above the cap (if your strategy has one), index dividends, and part of every year's growth to policy costs. Caps can change.

Who it's wrong for

Anyone without an emergency fund or protection in place, or who can't fund it for years. Early years build slowly.

Use your money without taking it out

Once your policy has built cash value, you can borrow against it. The cash value never leaves the policy, so it keeps growing as if you never touched it. You're borrowing the insurance company's money, with your cash value as the collateral.

1Build cash value

Fund the policy for a few years. Early years build slowly, because costs come out first.

2Borrow against it

No credit check, no application, no repayment schedule. You decide when and how much to pay back.

3Your money keeps growing

The full cash value keeps being credited, as if it never left. The loan charges interest, so your net growth is a little less.

Two jobs, two policies

Each job gets its own policy, so borrowing for an opportunity never cuts into the money meant for retirement.

Policy 1 · Your opportunity fund

Funded from what you save each month. Borrow for a business, a property, a car, or an emergency without asking a bank. Pay it back on your schedule, and use it again for the next opportunity.

Policy 2 · Your retirement income

Usually funded by moving money over from your current retirement accounts, such as an old 401(k) or IRA. In retirement, borrow a set amount each year as income. Loans are generally not taxed as income while the policy stays in force, and the cash value keeps being credited.

Money taken out of a 401(k) or IRA to fund a policy is taxed as a withdrawal, plus a penalty before 59½. On the flip side, any growth after the switch can be accessed tax-free through policy loans, as long as the policy stays in force.

What keeps it working

Pay at least what the policy needs to stay in force, and keep the loan well below the cash value.

What you give up

Loan interest, which trims your net growth. Unpaid, the loan and its interest come out of the death benefit.

Who it's wrong for

Anyone who would let the loan keep growing unchecked. If it outgrows the cash value, the policy can lapse, and that can create a tax bill.

This assumes the policy keeps crediting the full cash value while the loan is out. Many indexed policies offer loans that work this way; with others, the borrowed portion earns a different rate. Ask which kind of loan a policy has before you buy it.

Where this fits

A tool for later in the order, not the starting point

Goals first, then expenses, then the job each dollar needs to do, then the strategy. Products come last. An IUL usually makes sense only after the foundation is in place: steady cash flow, an emergency fund, protection for the people who depend on you, and high-interest debt under control.

Before you buy one from anyone, run the before-you-buy checklist, and ask to see the guaranteed column of the illustration next to the projected one.

Educational illustration only, using hypothetical numbers. It is not a policy illustration, offer, or projection. Indexed universal life is life insurance, not an investment in the market or a bank account. Crediting is subject to caps, participation rates, and floors set by the insurer, which can change. Policy charges reduce cash value and can cause it to fall even when the index credits 0%. Policy loans accrue interest, reduce the death benefit and cash value, and can cause the policy to lapse, which may result in taxes. Guarantees depend on the claims-paying ability of the insurer.
